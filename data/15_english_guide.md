# Thai Personal Income Tax — English Quick Guide

## Who must pay
Thai Personal Income Tax (PIT) applies to individuals who earn income from employment, business, or property located in Thailand, regardless of nationality or where payment is made. A person who stays in Thailand for 180 days or more in a calendar year is a tax resident. Tax residents must also pay tax on foreign-sourced income brought into Thailand. The tax year is the calendar year (1 January – 31 December).

## Filing thresholds
A single person with employment income only must file if annual income exceeds THB 120,000. A single person with other types of income must file if income exceeds THB 60,000. For married persons, the thresholds are THB 220,000 (employment income only) and THB 120,000 (other income).

## Tax forms
- PND 91: for individuals with employment income only (salary and wages).
- PND 90: for individuals with other income, such as freelance, rental, or business income.
- PND 94: half-year return for rental, professional, contracting, and business income received from January to June.

## Deadlines
Annual returns (PND 90/91) are due by 31 March of the following year for paper filing. Online filing at efiling.rd.go.th is usually extended to 8 April. The half-year return (PND 94) is due by 30 September (online by 8 October).

## Progressive tax rates (net income, THB)
- 0 – 150,000: exempt
- 150,001 – 300,000: 5%
- 300,001 – 500,000: 10%
- 500,001 – 750,000: 15%
- 750,001 – 1,000,000: 20%
- 1,000,001 – 2,000,000: 25%
- 2,000,001 – 5,000,000: 30%
- Over 5,000,000: 35%

## Common deductions and allowances
- Employment income: 50% expense deduction, capped at THB 100,000.
- Personal allowance: THB 60,000.
- Spouse with no income: THB 60,000.
- Child: THB 30,000 per child; THB 60,000 for the second child onward born in 2018 or later.
- Parents aged 60+ with income not exceeding THB 30,000: THB 30,000 per parent.
- Social security contributions: actual amount, up to THB 9,000 per year.
- Life insurance: up to THB 100,000; health insurance up to THB 25,000 (combined cap of THB 100,000).
- RMF: up to 30% of income, max THB 500,000. SSF: up to 30% of income, max THB 200,000. The retirement group (RMF, SSF, provident fund, pension insurance, NSF) has a combined cap of THB 500,000.
- Thai ESG Fund: up to 30% of income, max THB 300,000 (separate from the retirement cap).
- Home mortgage interest: up to THB 100,000.

## Payment and refunds
If the tax payable is THB 3,000 or more and the return is filed on time, the tax can be paid in three equal interest-free installments. Late payment incurs a surcharge of 1.5% per month, not exceeding the tax amount. Refunds are paid only to a PromptPay account linked to the taxpayer's national ID number, and must be claimed within 3 years.
